Worth checking in the drawings

  • There is no room left on floor area. The house is at 3,048 sf against 3,049 allowed (45% of the first 5,000 sf of the 7,664 sf lot plus 30% of the rest). Re-measured from the A2.4 blocks it is 3,047.7 sf against 3,049.3, so about a square foot and a half to spare. Any growth in the plan or the walls during construction documents would put it over.
  • The ADU is measured differently from the house. A2.4’s ADU blocks (A1 to A5) stop at the inside face of the unit’s outer walls on the street side, the south side and the porch side; the house blocks run to the outside face. Measured the way the house is, the ADU is about 783 sf, not 747. That still fits under the 800 sf that PAMC 18.09.040 (Table 2) leaves out of floor area and lot coverage, so the totals hold, but the cushion is about 17 sf, not 53.
  • Front-yard permeability is stated two ways. The cover sheet says 92% of the front setback is permeable; the diagram on A2.4 counts 224 sf impervious of 1,332, which is 83%. Both clear the 60% minimum in PAMC 18.12.040(h). The 92% would be right only if the 124 sf paved area in front of the ADU were left out.
  • One roof valley is drawn at an angle a 4 in 12 roof can’t make. West of the kitchen and dining roof, A2.3 draws a valley at about 63° to the walls, with a diagonal slope arrow; every other hip and valley on the sheet is at 45°, which is what two 4 in 12 roofs meeting at a right angle produce. Either that surface has another pitch, which the sheet doesn’t give, or the line is off. The model keeps 4 in 12 there.
  • Section 2 points to a sheet that isn’t in the set. The plans (A2.1, A2.2 and A2.3) tag the north–south section as 2/A4.2. It is drawn on A4.1, and there is no A4.2.
  • The sheet index doesn’t match the set. It lists A0.2 (site photos) and T-1 to T-3 (tree protection), which are not in this PDF, and leaves out A6.1, the window and door schedules, which is.

How the model was built. Walls, windows and doors were traced from the vector linework of A2.1 and A2.2, to within about an inch, with sizes from the schedules on A6.1. Heights come from A3.1, A3.2 and A4.1: the house floor at +58.4, a foot above average grade; a 9′-6″ first-floor plate; a 1′-2″ floor; a 9 ft second-floor plate; a 12 ft plate over the kitchen and dining room; door and window heads at 8 ft; clerestories from 9′-8″ to 11′-2″; the garage floor 6 in below the house floor. The roofs follow A2.3: 4 in 12 hips, a front gable over bedroom 2 with a lower front gable over bedroom 3, a rear gable over bedroom 1, 1′-6″ overhangs upstairs and 2 ft below; built that way, the model’s ridge lands at +83.7, as drawn. The covered patio has a flat roof 9′-2″ above the floor with four 4 ft skylights. Bedrooms 1 to 3 have vaulted ceilings along the dashed lines on A2.2. The 3D and Walk views are lit by a mid-October sun at about 11 am; the street side faces about 123°, which the drawings call east.

Assumed, because the drawings do not say: the colors (white stucco is in the keynotes; the charcoal roof, black frames and charcoal doors are not), the furniture and staging beyond what the plans draw (art, TVs, lamps, drapes, rugs and the things on tables and counters), the planting, the stair’s exact layout, and the neighbors’ heights (one-story houses with 9 ft plates, two-story with 18 ft; footprints from the cover sheet’s neighborhood context and, for 780 and 786, the site plan). Tree shapes are illustrations; trunk sizes and protection zones are from A1.1. Three small trees marked with an x on the site plan are not modeled. Walk puts your eye 5 ft 6 in above whatever you are standing on: the ground outside, the house floor a foot up indoors, the second floor upstairs.
